You tend to show up as

The High Achiever

“You always have it handled.”

At least, that's how it might look from the outside. But there's often more going on underneath.

This is a starting point, not a label. Most of us recognise ourselves in more than one of these patterns, because we learned different ways to stay safe at different times.

Not done until it's perfect

You've worked hard to build the life you have. From the outside, it looks like you've got everything you wanted. The career, the home, the body you train for.

You've achieved things you're proud of, set yourself high standards and become someone others can rely on to get things done.

And still, achieving more doesn't seem to make you any happier.

You get up early. You keep going until it's right. When something goes well, you feel a brief sense of relief, and then you're already looking at the next thing. A small win doesn't really count. It's not done until it's perfect, and somehow it's never quite perfect.

I know this one from the inside. I chose the paths where I knew I could do well, and I did. I reached director level. On paper it looked solid. But I wasn't lit up by any of it, and I didn't understand why.

Built for safety

Maybe somewhere along the way, being good at things helped you feel accepted or valued. Maybe it was the way people responded when you did well. Maybe getting it right meant nobody looked too closely at anything else.

I don't think that's a flaw. It's understandable that we hold on to ways of behaving that have helped us feel safe, accepted or loved.

Am I enough now?

Sometimes, underneath all that achievement, there's a question: Am I enough?

Reaching the next goal might answer it for a while. But then the feeling fades, and there's something else to achieve, something else to prove.

It can cost you the joy of the thing you're making while you make it. It can cost you evenings, friendships, the moment in front of you.

I described it once like this. It's very carefully supported by the performance. If I move one of those little pillars, everything can come crashing down.

So you don't move them. You keep everything standing, and you call it doing well.

Enjoying what you've built

I don't think the answer is to stop caring about doing things well. That's part of who you are, and it's built a lot that's good.

But something shifts when you start noticing what drives it. You might find you don't need it as much as you once did. You might find out what it feels like to enjoy the life you've worked so hard to build.

What if your worth was never something you had to prove in the first place?

Need more support?

Gay Men Connect is a space for connection and conversation, not a substitute for professional mental health support. If you're struggling, please speak to a qualified mental health professional. If you're in immediate danger, contact your local emergency services.

  • Samaritans: call free on 116 123, any time, day or night
  • Switchboard LGBT+: call free on 0800 0119 100
  • In the US: call or text 988
  • Pink Therapy: a directory of therapists who work with gender and sexual diversity, at pinktherapy.com
  • If you're in danger right now, call 999, or your local emergency number.
