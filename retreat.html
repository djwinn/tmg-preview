A weekend where you don't have to perform

You don't need to come prepared with anything to share, or with anything profound to say. You can share as much or as little of yourself as you feel comfortable with.

That's the whole point. You don't have to perform here. You can just be.

There are no expectations to open up, no pressure to have a breakthrough, and no demands to be anyone other than yourself.

What the weekend is like

We'll connect through honest conversations, movement, nature and guided experiences such as meditation, breathwork and sound baths. There will be shared meals, time outdoors, time for your own reflection, and space to simply be.

We'll gather around the fire, as men have done for centuries, sharing stories, experiences and moments of reflection. What you bring to that fire is entirely up to you.

It's a weekend to connect with other men beyond the physical, free from sex, drugs and alcohol.

Together, we'll create a space built on respect, confidentiality and genuine support. A space where we listen to understand rather than judge, where we don't try to fix each other or impose our own beliefs.

And perhaps, through simply being ourselves and allowing others to do the same, we'll find a deeper sense of connection, belonging and brotherhood.

Why I'm putting this together

I'm in the middle of this work myself. I've sat around fires with men I'd never met, and I've felt what it's like to stop managing how I come across, even for a weekend. Those weekends meant a great deal to me, and I wanted to create the same chance for gay, bi, trans and queer men.

This is the first retreat I'm hosting, and I've planned every part of it with care: the place, the shape of each day, a small group of ten. What happens between the men who come is the part nobody can script, and that's what makes it worth coming for. I'd love you to be one of them.

What you might leave with

I can't promise you what the weekend will mean to you. I can tell you what I hope.

  • That you leave with a smile on your face, feeling seen and heard, perhaps for the first time.
  • That you leave with a little more understanding of who you are and what you want from life, and a curiosity to keep exploring.
  • That you leave with a sense of belonging. New friendships, community, maybe even a sense of brotherhood.
  • And that you go back to everyday life feeling a little more connected to yourself and to other men, with the knowledge that you don't have to do it all alone.

Who it's for, and who it isn't

It's for gay, bi, trans and queer men who'd like a weekend with other men where nothing has to be performed. You don't need to have everything figured out to come.

It isn't a party, a dating weekend or a quick fix. It isn't a substitute for therapy. And it's a weekend without sex, drugs or alcohol, so you'll need to be comfortable with that.

What I ask is that you respect other men's experiences, keep what's shared confidential, and take part in your own way.

The place

Hill Farm is in Tintern, near Chepstow, in the Wye Valley. It's under two hours by train from London Paddington. The nearest station is Chepstow, about 15 minutes away by taxi.

You'll sleep in a shared yurt, with two to four men in each. On site there's a studio overlooking the valley, a woodland fire circle, a shower block, and a wood-fired hot tub, sauna and cold plunge.

Need more support?

Gay Men Connect is a space for connection and conversation, not a substitute for professional mental health support. If you're struggling, please speak to a qualified mental health professional. If you're in immediate danger, contact your local emergency services.

  • Samaritans: call free on 116 123, any time, day or night
  • Switchboard LGBT+: call free on 0800 0119 100
  • In the US: call or text 988
  • Pink Therapy: a directory of therapists who work with gender and sexual diversity, at pinktherapy.com
  • If you're in danger right now, call 999, or your local emergency number.
