From the outside, life looks pretty good

Many of us built lives that, from the outside, make it look like we have everything together. The career. The home. The friends, the partner, the holidays, the photos.

And then we reach midlife and wonder: how did I get here?

I was surrounded by people and still didn't feel truly known. Maybe you know that feeling too.

Am I allowed to ask if I'm happy, when I have so much to be grateful for?

I think we are.

Coming out was the first step

Growing up, many of us learned that something about us wasn't acceptable. Sometimes we were told directly. More often it was the looks, the jokes, the way people talked about men like us. So we learned to hide parts of ourselves. We became who we thought we needed to be to feel safe, accepted and loved.

Then we came out, and the hiding was supposed to stop.

For a lot of us, it didn't. The ways we learned to protect ourselves followed us into our careers, our friendships and our relationships.

When we come out of the closet, where do we go? Nobody is there to welcome us. Nobody is there to guide us, or help us heal from the damage done by years of hiding ourselves, from the internalised homophobia, from the shame and from the fear of being ourselves.

We talk about the gay community, but how many of us have found somewhere we can be genuinely known?

What it's like to talk with Heni

Who I am

I'm Heni. When I started That Midlife Gay, I wasn't trying to start a movement. I was trying to figure myself out.

I write and host a podcast on Substack about shame, people-pleasing, getting older, and why, after being out for so long, I was still editing parts of myself. And I've found something through those conversations. When one queer man talks honestly, another often recognises himself.

I'm not writing from the other side of this. I'm still figuring things out myself. I'm sharing what I find as I go, for men who see themselves in it.

I'm a Jay Shetty certified Life & Success Coach and I'm also in training as a men's work facilitator.

Need more support?

Gay Men Connect is a space for connection and conversation, not a substitute for professional mental health support. If you're struggling, please speak to a qualified mental health professional. If you're in immediate danger, contact your local emergency services.

  • Samaritans: call free on 116 123, any time, day or night
  • Switchboard LGBT+: call free on 0800 0119 100
  • In the US: call or text 988
  • Pink Therapy: a directory of therapists who work with gender and sexual diversity, at pinktherapy.com
  • If you're in danger right now, call 999, or your local emergency number.
