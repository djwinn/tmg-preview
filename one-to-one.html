How it works

We meet for twelve 50-minute sessions over three months, on Zoom.

Between our sessions there are reflection exercises, journaling prompts and practical things to try in everyday life, so you can see your patterns as they happen. The work happens beyond our conversations, not only during them. You can message me on WhatsApp between sessions. I reply Monday to Friday during business hours, within 24 hours.

Over the three months, we move from understanding the beliefs and patterns that have shaped your life to exploring what you want the next chapter to look like. Along the way you create your own Life Guide. It isn't an action plan full of generic steps. It's yours, written by you, something you can return to long after our sessions end.

About me

I'm Heni, founder of That Midlife Gay, a Jay Shetty Certified Life & Success Coach, and currently training as a men's work facilitator.

My professional background spans 20 years in business and marketing, including 11 years as a director. Since 2020, I've also built and run my own photography business. Those experiences have taught me a great deal about working with people, listening, building trust and navigating change.

My approach to coaching begins with curiosity. I help men explore the beliefs, behaviours and patterns that have shaped their lives, understand which ones still serve them and consider what they might want to do differently.

Through honest conversations, thoughtful questions and practical reflection, we work towards greater self-understanding and clarity. But understanding is only part of the process. I'm interested in how we take what we learn about ourselves into our relationships, decisions and everyday lives.

As a circle facilitator, my role is different. I'm there to create and hold a respectful, supportive space where men can speak openly, listen to one another and explore their experiences without judgement or pressure to share.

Whether we're working one to one or sitting together in a circle, my approach is grounded in empathy, self-compassion and the belief that meaningful change begins with understanding ourselves more deeply.
